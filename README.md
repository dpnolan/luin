## Historic Irish Rockets

Few people know that some of the world’s most advanced early rockets were designed, built and tested in Dublin.

The Lúin Project tells that story.

It explores the rockets associated with Robert Emmet and the 1803 United Irishmen rising, their links to Indian rocket technology, and their possible influence on Britain’s later Congreve rockets.

This is a story of science, engineering, rebellion, empire and memory.

It deserves to be better known.

---

## Why It Matters

Ireland is famous for its writers, rebels and political history.

It is less often remembered as a place of scientific and engineering ambition.

Robert Emmet’s rocket work shows another side of Irish history: inventive, technical, experimental and international.

The Lúin Project aims to recover that story and share it with the public.

---

## Robert Emmet and Dublin Rocketry

Robert Emmet was a brilliant Trinity College Dublin student with interests in science, politics, military history and engineering.

In preparation for the 1803 rising, Emmet and his associates appear to have worked on rockets and other military technologies in secret.

Because the work was clandestine, few documents and artefacts survive.

That makes the project both difficult and exciting.

We are using historical research, engineering analysis, digital modelling and physical replicas to understand what Emmet’s rockets may have looked like, how they worked, and how they fit into the wider history of rocketry.

---

## The Indian Connection

Emmet’s rockets did not appear from nowhere.

One likely source of inspiration was the powerful rocket technology used by Indian states against the British in the late eighteenth century.

Tipu Sultan of Mysore is especially famous for using rockets in war. His forces fought the British in southern India, including in the region around modern Bangalore, now one of India’s great technology centres.

Other Indian powers, including the Marathas and Scindias, also used rockets.

British officers, veterans and published military works helped carry knowledge of these technologies across Europe.

The Lúin Project explores how this Indian rocket tradition may have influenced Emmet’s work in Dublin.

---

## From Emmet to Congreve

After the failed 1803 rising, British authorities seized materials, notes and equipment connected with Emmet and his associates.

Soon afterwards, Britain developed its own military rockets under Sir William Congreve.

Congreve rockets became famous during the wars against Napoleon and the United States.

They were used in the attack on Washington in 1814 and later at Baltimore, where Francis Scott Key saw “the rockets’ red glare” — words now sung in the American national anthem.

The Lúin Project asks an important historical question:

**What role did Irish and Indian technology play in the rise of British military rocketry?**

## What We Are Building

The project will create a new public account of this forgotten history.

Our work includes:

* Researching surviving records on Emmet’s rockets.
* Studying Indian, Irish and British rocket artefacts.
* Creating accurate CAD models and digital reconstructions.
* Producing safe display replicas for museums and public events.
* Explaining the science, engineering and history behind early rockets.
* Sharing the story through talks, exhibitions, video and online resources.

The goal is to make this history visible, understandable and memorable.

---

## Public Engagement

The Lúin Project is designed for historians, engineers, students, museums, schools and the wider public.

We want people to see that Ireland has a deeper scientific and technological history than is often recognised.

Display models, digital reconstructions, videos and public demonstrations can help bring this story to life.

The project also connects naturally with Ireland’s growing student rocketry and aerospace community.

Ireland has the talent to take part in Europe’s new space age.

It also has a forgotten rocket history of its own.

---

## Public Diplomacy

Robert Emmet is remembered every year as a patriot and revolutionary.

He should also be remembered as part of the history of science and engineering.

The story also links Ireland, India, Britain and the United States through one remarkable technology: the rocket.

The Lúin Project will encourage commemorations, exhibitions and international partnerships that recognise this shared history.

If Sir William Congreve can be remembered in the history of rocketry, then Robert Emmet and Tipu Sultan deserve to be remembered too.

---

## The Lúin Project

Lúin is named after the legendary self-moving spear of Irish myth, one of the treasures of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

It is a fitting name for a project about Ireland’s forgotten place in the story of rockets.

The Lúin Project brings together history, engineering and public imagination.

Its purpose is simple:

**To recover Ireland’s lost rocket story — and share it with the world.**
